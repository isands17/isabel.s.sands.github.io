Overview

I work at the intersection of theoretical particle physics and astrophysics, developing models and simulations that connect fundamental physics to observable signatures across cosmic scales. My projects span cosmic ray phenomenology in galaxy simulations, the indirect detection of dark matter through gravitational and radiative probes, and the cosmological signatures of physics beyond the standard ΛCDM model.

I frequently take on new projects as compelling questions arise, so this page reflects my current and recent work rather than a fixed research program.

Astro-particle physics & galaxy formation

Modeling cosmic ray interactions and diffuse emission in MHD galaxy formation simulations

Cosmic rays are a dynamically important component of the interstellar medium, yet their transport, interactions, and radiative losses are challenging to incorporate self-consistently into galaxy formation simulations. I develop and implement cosmic ray physics in magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) cosmological simulations, with a focus on predicting observable gamma-ray and radio signatures of cosmic ray populations in galaxies across a range of environments and redshifts.

The galactic center gamma-ray excess and time-dependent cosmic ray transport

The excess of GeV gamma-ray emission toward the galactic center — detected by Fermi-LAT — remains one of the most intriguing unresolved signals in indirect detection. I investigate whether time-dependent cosmic ray injection and transport in a realistic galactic potential can reproduce the morphology and spectrum of this excess without invoking dark matter annihilation, building models constrained by multi-wavelength observations.

Fermi bubbles in cosmological simulations

The Fermi bubbles — symmetric lobes of gamma-ray emission extending ~50° above and below the galactic plane — may trace either past AGN activity or a starburst-driven galactic wind. I study whether cosmological MHD simulations of Milky Way-mass galaxies can self-consistently produce bubble-like structures with the observed gamma-ray luminosity and morphology, and what this implies for the star formation and feedback history of the Galaxy.

Astro-particle physics

Indirect detection of dark matter from gravitational effects

Dark matter reveals itself primarily through gravity. While direct and collider searches probe the particle nature of dark matter, gravitational probes — rotation curves, velocity dispersions, lensing, and satellite counts — constrain its distribution on galactic and sub-galactic scales. I study how these observables can be used to distinguish dark matter models and what systematic uncertainties limit their constraining power.

The limits of galaxy rotation curves as probes of dark matter

Rotation curves have long been the canonical evidence for dark matter halos, yet their interpretation is complicated by baryonic feedback, non-circular motions, and mass–anisotropy degeneracies. I analyze the theoretical and observational limits on what rotation curves can and cannot tell us about the dark matter density profile, the core–cusp problem, and the diversity of rotation curve shapes seen in dwarf galaxies.

Cosmology

Simulating beyond ΛCDM

The ΛCDM model is remarkably successful on large scales, yet persistent tensions — including the Hubble tension, the S8 discrepancy, and small-scale structure anomalies — motivate exploring extensions and alternatives. I develop theoretical frameworks and simulation tools to study models in which dark matter has non-trivial self-interactions, ultra-light fields, or other properties that modify the growth of structure.

Long-range self-interactions in the dark sector

Dark matter with long-range self-interactions — mediated by a light dark photon or scalar — can produce velocity-dependent cross-sections that modify halo structure on dwarf scales while remaining consistent with cluster constraints. I study the cosmological and astrophysical signatures of these models, including their effect on the halo mass function, density profiles, and the formation of dark matter bound states at late times.

Perturbation theory for ultra-light dark matter

Ultra-light axion-like particles — with masses ~10−22 eV — exhibit a de Broglie wavelength on astrophysically relevant scales, suppressing small-scale structure and producing quantum interference patterns in the density field. I develop analytical perturbation theory methods for this "fuzzy dark matter" regime, connecting predictions for the matter power spectrum and halo profiles to observables from the Lyman-α forest and high-redshift galaxy surveys.
